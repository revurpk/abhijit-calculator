Tarabala — Mitra & Parama-Mitra (9-Tara compatibility)
Marriage compatibility (Ashtakuta · Shri B.V. Raman)
Guidance · adapted from Shri B.V. Raman's Muhurtha (Electional Astrology)
तदेव लग्नं सुदिनं तदेव ताराबलं चन्द्रबलं तदेव ।
विद्याबलं दैवबलं तदेव लक्ष्मीपते तेंऽघ्रियुगं स्मरामि ॥
"That alone is the auspicious ascendant, that alone the auspicious day; that alone the strength of stars, that alone the strength of the Moon. That alone the strength of knowledge, that alone divine grace — that very moment when I meditate on your divine feet, O Lord Lakshmi-pati."
In this spirit. The verse teaches that the truest bala — strength of moment, star, Moon, knowledge — arises from sincere intention and remembrance of the Divine. No timing chart can confer success on action lacking integrity; equally, an action undertaken with devotion, clear purpose, and ethical care draws auspiciousness to itself even when the classical indicators are mixed. The rules below are aids to alignment, not verdicts. Where they signal challenges, treat them as invitations to greater care, prayer, and preparation — not as closed doors.
  • Tarabala (strength of star). Counting the day's star from the Janma star, days landing on Sampat (2), Kshema (4), Sadhana (6), Mitra (8), and Parama-Mitra (9/0) carry favourable currents. Vipat (3), Pratyak (5), and Naidhana (7) ask for greater care during major undertakings — long journeys, marriage, business launches — and can be balanced by attention to Lagna, devotional preparation, and (if needed) postponement to a smoother window. Janma (count 1) ordinarily warrants caution; on one's own birth star the first 7 ghatis (~3 h) of the day are traditionally set aside.
  • Janma Nakshatra — when it is acceptable. Shri B.V. Raman (Ch. III, end) lists important events for which the Janma star itself is favourable without exception: nuptials, sacrifices (yajna), first feeding (annaprasana), agriculture, upanayanam (sacred-thread ceremony), coronation, buying land, and learning the alphabet (vidyarambha). For other categories — war, shaving, medical treatment, travel, and marriage — the Janma star is less suited; with thoughtful preparation it can still be navigated. For a woman, the Janma Nakshatra is fully favourable for marriage.
  • Cycle (Paryaya) softening. The classical adverse indications apply in full only in cycle 1 (counts 1–9). In cycle 2 (10–18) they soften to about half; in cycle 3 (19–27) they are almost negligible. Most "unfavourable" stars a person will encounter in life sit in cycle 2 or 3, where intention and care easily outweigh the residual indication.
  • Chandrabala. The Moon at the chosen time should not occupy the 6th, 8th, or 12th rashi from the person's Janma Rasi. When this isn't possible, prayer and devotion help draw the lunar mind into harmony with the moment.
  • Marriage compatibility. The eight kutas read together describe the texture of a union — no single score determines its fate. The traditional 18 / 36 threshold is a useful waypoint; Shri B.V. Raman cautions in Ch. IX that longevity and the 7th-house strength of each chart matter more than the kuta count. Where Nadi or 6/8 Bhakuta show challenges, classical tradition prescribes parihara (remedies): pre-marital rituals, gemstone or mantra adjustments, charitable acts. Many marriages with low kuta totals flourish because the couple cultivates shared intention.
  • A liberal note from the sages. Shri B.V. Raman closes his Muhurtha with a striking concession: "The sages appear to have taken a very liberal view of things especially in regard to marriage. For instance they say that if the boy and the girl like each other in their first meeting, that should also receive first consideration." Mutual recognition and willing affection are themselves an auspicious indication that no chart can outrank.
  • Days/stars usually set aside. The 4th, 8th, 9th, 12th and 14th tithis (both pakshas); Bharani and Krittika for marriage; Tuesday and Saturday for new ventures (unless the Lagna is strong); the nakshatra's own tyajya (varjyam) window. These are tendencies, not absolutes — see the Varjyam window for the precise sub-hour slice rather than skipping the whole day.
  • When the indications are mixed. Three classical neutralisers carry weight beyond the daily panchanga: (i) a strong, well-placed Lagna at the moment of action, (ii) Abhijit muhurta around solar noon (which overrides most negative influences), and (iii) the spirit of the shloka above — work begun with sincere remembrance, clarity of intent, and surrender of fruit to the Divine attracts its own auspiciousness, often quietly transcending what the chart appeared to forbid.
  • Caveats. Muhurta is preventive and supportive, not a guarantee — and it is not a verdict against any soul. Its task is to not add to existing afflictions and to gently amplify what is already good. For weighty decisions, consult an experienced astrologer who can weigh the full birth chart, dashas, and family context alongside these indications.

Hindu Panchanga Calculator
A self-contained single-file web app — no server, no framework, no build step. All five panchanga elements (tithi, nakshatra, yoga, karana, vara) plus lunar masa, eclipse, moudhyam, festivals, and auspiciousness scores are computed live in your browser using JavaScript astronomical models. Results may differ from printed almanacs by minutes to a day depending on the element.

Panchanga Elements
All five angas on the Panchanga tab are evaluated at local sunrise for the selected date and location — the reference moment used by traditional printed almanacs. The calendar grid uses noon as a representative snapshot for quick browsing.
Tithi Moon−Sun elongation ÷ 12°
diff = nm(mlong − slong); tIdx = ⌊diff / 12⌋ → 0–29
0–14: Shukla Pratipada → Purnima  ·  15–29: Krishna Pratipada → Amavasya  ·  duration ~19–26 h
Nakshatra sidereal Moon ÷ 13°20′
nRaw = nm(mlong − ayan) / (360/27); nIdx = ⌊nRaw⌋; pada = ⌊(nRaw − nIdx)×4⌋ + 1
27 nakshatras × 4 padas = 108 navamshas  ·  average duration ~27 h 13 min
Yoga sidereal (Sun+Moon) sum ÷ 13°20′
yIdx = ⌊nm(slong + mlong − 2·ayan) / (360/27)⌋ % 27
27 yogas  ·  ayanamsha subtracted twice (once per body)  ·  duration ~12–26 h, advances faster than nakshatra
Karana Moon−Sun elongation ÷ 6° (½ tithi)
kIdx = ⌊diff / 6⌋ (0–59 per lunation)
11 types: 7 movable (Bava, Balava, Kaulava, Taitila, Garija, Vanija, Vishti) cycle from kIdx 1–56  ·  4 fixed at month boundaries (Kimstughna, Shakuni, Chatushpada, Naga)
Vara weekday from calendar date
vara = date.getDay() (0 = Sun … 6 = Sat)
Hindu vara is sunrise-to-sunrise; date.getDay() is correct for the Panchanga tab (which samples at sunrise) and for daytime hours. It may be off by one only for the narrow pre-sunrise window on the calendar grid.
Lunar Month (Masa) — Amanta System
A lunar month runs new-moon to new-moon and is named for the Sankranti (solar rashi entry) that falls within it. Let r₀ = Sun's sidereal rashi at the current new moon, r₁ = rashi at the next new moon.
r₀ vs r₁TypeNameSignificance
r₀ ≠ r₁Normal (or Nija)HM[r₁]Standard muhurtha rules apply
r₀ = r₁Adhika (intercalary)HM[r₂]Avoid new ventures; merit for religious acts
Nija = the regular month immediately following an adhika month. Adhika months occur ~every 2.5–3 years, when the Sun stays in one rashi across two consecutive new moons (near aphelion, ~July). 2026 has Adhika Jyeshtha (June).
Ritu — solar & lunar seasons
Two parallel notions of "season" coexist. Souramana ritu is solar — based on the Sun's sidereal rashi, 2 rashis per ritu. Chandramana ritu is lunar — based on the lunar masa, 2 masas per ritu. They can disagree by up to a month near Sankranti.

Lagna east-horizon ecliptic intersection
L_asc = atan2(cos(RAMC), −(sin(ε)·tan(φ) + cos(ε)·sin(RAMC)))
RAMC = Local Sidereal Time. atan2 signs are chosen to pick the rising intersection (east), not the setting one. Verified: at sunrise, Lagna ≈ Sun.

9-Tara · Mitra & Parama-Mitra Shri B.V. Raman Ch. III
For the reference nakshatra B, the 9-Tara cycle classifies every other nakshatra N by (count − 1) mod 9 where count = ((N − B + 27) mod 27) + 1. Mitra stars sit at offsets B+7, B+16, B+25; Parama-Mitra at B+8, B+17, B+26. Today's Moon nakshatra is also classified against the reference to show the current Tarabala (Sampat/Vipat/Kshema/Pratyak/Sadhana/Naidhana/Mitra/Parama-Mitra) and which cycle (Paryaya) softens it.

Viewing guide where to actually look
A "weather permitting" panel converts each body's ecliptic position to altitude (above horizon) and azimuth (compass bearing N/NE/E/…/NW). Bodies below the horizon show their next rise time (within 24 h). Mercury/Venus within 10° of the Sun are flagged "lost in glare". The Sun's altitude is classified into day / civil / nautical / astronomical twilight / night. The Moon's current nakshatra is shown with its principal stars (e.g. Rohini → Aldebaran) and a pointer to where the Moon is in the sky — so the abstract nakshatra index becomes a visible target you can find.

Security & Privacy
Everything you see is computed in your browser. The app has no backend, makes no network requests after the initial page load, and stores nothing across sessions (no cookies, no localStorage, no telemetry). Your location, dates, and searches never leave this tab.
DefenceWhat it does
Content-Security-PolicyRestricts script/style/font/img sources. connect-src 'none' blocks any runtime exfiltration. frame-ancestors 'none' prevents clickjacking.
Subresource IntegrityTabler Icons CDN pinned to a SHA-384 hash — CDN compromise → browser refuses the file.
Referrer policyno-referrer globally, so font/icon requests do not leak the page URL.
HTML escapingAn esc() helper wraps every interpolation of LOC.name into HTML so a future code path that brings in untrusted location strings can't inject markup.
Input validationLat/lon range-checked; NaN/Infinity rejected. Date inputs verified before use. parseInt always with radix and finite-number guard.
URL parameters?loc=Name is the only accepted URL parameter; matched strictly (case-insensitive) against the static CITIES list. Unknown names are ignored; raw lat/lon from the URL are never accepted; the URL string is never interpolated into HTML.
GeolocationNot auto-requested on load (cold prompt is poor UX and often auto-denied). User clicks "Auto-detect" explicitly; 10 s timeout; error codes translated to readable messages. Coordinates never leave the browser.
Cache boundingAll caches soft-capped at 5000 entries; oldest 1000 dropped when exceeded.
Residual risks: inline event handlers prevent the strictest CSP variant (no 'unsafe-inline'); Google Fonts cannot be SRI-pinned because its CSS varies per user-agent. If you self-host, both concerns disappear.
